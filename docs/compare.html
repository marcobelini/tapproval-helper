Approving Claude Code away from your desk: the options compared

Written by the maker of Tapproval, so read it with that in mind. Every claim about another product links to its source; facts as of 24 October 2026.

Claude Code stops and waits whenever it needs permission. Several tools now let you answer from somewhere other than the terminal. They differ more than their descriptions suggest: some need an account and a server in the middle, some need a particular plan, and only a few reach the Apple Watch.

Which one fits

  • You already pay for Claude Pro or Max and mostly carry your phone: start with Anthropic's Remote Control. It is built in and needs nothing installed.
  • You want Android, the web or a desktop client: Happy or Omnara. Happy is the most widely used. Omnara has moved towards managed agents in its own cloud.
  • You want to build your own: claude-watch (watch and Mac on the same Wi-Fi) or Chums (Claude Code and Codex, a hub on your own Mac) are good open-source starts. Both are source code you sign and build yourself.
  • You want a push on your phone and nothing on your wrist: claude-remote-approver turns each prompt into an ntfy notification with Approve and Deny buttons, free and open source; Pushary does the same as a hosted service at $9.99 a month.
  • You want the watch to be the whole interface, with no account, no server of ours in the middle, and each prompt marked with how risky it is: that is what Tapproval is for. By default it approves nothing on its own; every card waits for your tap.

Also seen

  • Taphaptic: lifecycle notifications from Claude Code and Codex to a watch app, through a relay and Apple's push service. Its README says prompts, transcripts and tool arguments are not sent, only a minimal event. MIT. Whether it is on the App Store is not stated on the page.
  • claude-remote-approver: a hook that pushes each permission prompt to the ntfy app on iOS or Android, with Approve, Always Approve and Deny buttons; falls back to the terminal after 120 seconds. No account; Node.js. MIT.
  • Pushary: a hosted service that installs Claude Code hooks and pushes the question to your phone, "Tap yes or no from your lock screen". iPhone, Android and Mac; $9.99 a month after a three-day trial. No watch mentioned.

Who Remote Control's own documentation rules out

Anthropic's Remote Control is the built-in way to answer from a phone, and for many people the right one. Its requirements page also says, in its own words, who cannot use it, and that is where the independent tools still have a job:

  • "available on Pro, Max, Team, and Enterprise plans. API keys are not supported."
  • Not available when you use "Amazon Bedrock, Google Cloud's Agent Platform, or Microsoft Foundry", or point ANTHROPIC_BASE_URL at a gateway or proxy.
  • Not available with CLAUDE_CODE_DISABLE_NONESSENTIAL_TRAFFIC or DISABLE_GROWTHBOOK set.
  • "While Remote Control is connected, the session transcript, including your messages, Claude's responses, and tool activity, is stored on Anthropic servers." Organizations with Zero Data Retention or the HIPAA configuration cannot enable it.
  • The page names claude.ai/code, the Claude app for iOS and Android, Desktop and VS Code. It does not mention the Apple Watch; the phone's notification is mirrored to the wrist as any notification is.

Tapproval sits on the other side of each line: it works with an API key or a gateway, keeps the conversation between your computer and your watch, and puts the risk mark on the card before you tap. It is not a replacement for Remote Control's typing and reading on a phone; it is the wrist half.

How the approval actually travels

All of these rely on the same thing underneath: Claude Code's hooks, or its own Remote Control channel. A tool that answers from outside the terminal uses the PermissionRequest hook, and the decision it prints has a shape that is easy to get wrong. A wrong shape is ignored without an error. How the hook works covers the exact shape.
